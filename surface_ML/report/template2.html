Paved or unpaved: two models compared and combined

We ran two road-surface classifiers on US aerial photos: jdalrym2's MaskCNN, which we trained here, and GIScience's SUSformer. We scored each on its own, then tried several ways to combine them. All numbers come from the same 11,792 held-out roads, in areas neither model trained on.

95.8%our MaskCNN alone
97.2%SUSformer, retrained on US photos
97.6%both models + OSM tags, every road answered
99.9%same, on the 80% of roads it is most sure about

SUSformer as released scores only 85% on US photos. After retraining it on our 44k US training roads it beats our model by 1.4 points. Combining both models with OSM tags adds another 0.3 points and gives the most reliable confidence scores. 100% is not reachable because the answers come from OSM tags, and some of those are wrong.

1. Each model on its own

SUSformer was trained on drone photos about ten times sharper than NAIP, so we first ran it unchanged. It scored 84–85%. It calls many gravel and compacted roads paved, getting only 57–63% of them right. Wider close-ups help a little.

ModelAll held-outReal US mix

SUSformer as released, 30 m close-ups85.1%86.2%
Our MaskCNN (3 saved versions × 4 flipped copies)95.8%94.6%
SUSformer retrained on US photos, 30 m close-ups, 4 flipped copies97.2%96.8%

We then retrained SUSformer on the same 44,083 US training roads our model used, starting from its released weights and keeping its own training recipe. The first pass only trains the final layer. After that the whole network trains, and accuracy levels off at about 97% by pass 5.

Averaging SUSformer's three best saved versions gave 97.26% against 97.24% for the single best, so we kept one version. Both models' best versions were chosen on this held-out set, which flatters each by a similar small amount.

2. Where each model does better

The retrained SUSformer is better on every unpaved surface and on asphalt and concrete. Our model is slightly better on paving stones and bricks, which it saw more of in its 8-surface training. Roads tagged only surface=paved are the hardest for both. Many of those look like wrong tags.

3. Combining the two

The models agree on 96.1% of roads, and are 98.4% right where they agree. On the 456 roads where they disagree, SUSformer is right 68% of the time and ours 32%. We tried three fixed rules and four learned combinations. The learned ones are small gradient-boosted models, scored by holding out whole 1° squares of the map so they cannot memorise places.

“Real US mix” is the 4,353 held-out roads sampled in proportion to how common each surface is in the US. It is mostly asphalt, generic unpaved and gravel. On that mix, SUSformer alone is as good as any combination.

The “both must say paved” rule

This rule does not give the best overall accuracy, but it is the strictest about calling a road paved. Use it when calling a dirt road paved costs more than missing a paved road.

MethodSays “paved” and is rightReal paved roads foundReal unpaved roads found
MaskCNN alone95.7%96.2%95.4%
SUSformer alone97.8%96.9%97.6%

Paved if either says paved95.0%98.3%94.4%
Both + OSM tags (learned)97.9%97.5%97.7%

The learned combination can be tuned the same way: raise the probability needed before it says “paved”, and it trades found paved roads for fewer false ones, usually more efficiently than the fixed rule.

4. Answering only when confident

Each method outputs a probability. Sort roads from most to least confident, answer for the top share, and flag the rest as uncertain. This is where the combination helps most: at 80% of roads answered, it is wrong about once per 700 roads, against once per 150 for our model alone.

Earlier experiments on our model

Before adding SUSformer, we tried to push MaskCNN alone. Its training curve:

ApproachAll held-out
Single prediction95.5%
+ average over 4 flipped copies95.7%
+ average over 3 saved versions95.8%
OSM tags alone, no image86.8%
Image + OSM tags + location + edit age96.4%

Tag age. Recently edited roads are scored more accurately: 96.6–97.0% for edits within a year, against 94.8% for roads untouched for 8+ years and 94.4% for roads edited only once. To test whether newer tags make better training labels, we retrained four times on 7,220 roads each, changing only how they were chosen:

Filtering training roads by edit date, or removing tags an earlier model disagreed with, made no measurable difference. More training roads and a sharper view of the road surface matter far more.

What the remaining errors look like

In a review of the top 24, about 10 looked like wrong tags and 4 were hidden roads. Measured accuracy therefore understates true accuracy by up to about a point.

Recommendation

  • Best accuracy: run both models and combine them with the OSM tags: 97.6% on every road, 99.9% on the 80% it is most confident about.
  • Simplest good option: retrained SUSformer alone: 97.2%, and 99.7% at 80% answered.
  • Fewest false “paved” calls: “paved only if both say paved”. 98.7% of its paved answers are right, at the cost of missing about 5% of paved roads.
  • Check the DINOv3 License before using SUSformer's weights in a product or redistributing them.
  • Decide the final class grouping before labelling the whole US. Changing it means retraining both models, about one day for SUSformer and one day for MaskCNN starting from the current weights.
