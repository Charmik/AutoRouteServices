Paved or unpaved: what worked

We trained jdalrym2's road surface classifier from scratch on this machine, then tried six ways to push paved/unpaved accuracy higher. Every number here comes from the same 11,792 held-out roads, in areas the model never trained on.

95.8%image model alone, 3 saved versions × 4 flipped copies
96.4%image model + OSM road tags + location
99.6%on the 80% of roads it is most sure about; the other 20% flagged “uncertain”

The paper claims 92–95%. Our plain model clears the top of that range, and adding OSM context lifts it further. 100% is out of reach because the answers we score against are OSM tags, and some of those are wrong.

What “paved” and “unpaved” mean here

The label is the road's OSM surface= tag, grouped into two classes. Roads with no surface tag, rare surfaces (wood, metal), private roads, driveways and parking aisles are left out.

ClassOSM surface values
pavedasphalt, concrete (incl. plates, lanes), paved, chipseal, paving_stones, bricks, sett, cobblestone
unpavedunpaved, gravel, fine_gravel, compacted, dirt, earth, ground, sand, grass, mud, pebblestone, rock

This grouping can be changed later to match your project, for example to give compacted gravel its own class. That needs a short retrain, not new downloads.

Data

  • 55,875 image chips, 256 × 256 m each, centred on one OSM road. Paved and unpaved are about even.
  • 35,072 chips are from the earlier 8-surface run, which has extra bricks and paving stones. 20,803 are new, sampled in proportion to how common each tag is in the US, so mostly asphalt, generic unpaved and gravel.
  • Split by area: 44,083 training chips and 11,792 held-out chips. Whole 0.1° grid cells go to one side or the other, so a held-out road never has a neighbouring segment in training.
  • The model's training run started from the 8-surface model. Its held-out areas were kept held out here, so no road it trained on is scored.

1. Main model: training on paved/unpaved

The 8-surface model already scored 93.9% paved/unpaved on its own smaller test set. Retraining it on just the two classes with all 44k chips gave a steady climb. Gains had shrunk to 0.02 points per 4–7 hour pass by pass 8, so we stopped there.

2–3. Averaging predictions and adding OSM tags

Both of these happen after training, so they cost minutes, not days. The tag model is a small gradient-boosted classifier that takes the image model's score plus tags that untagged roads also have. It was scored by holding out whole 1° squares so it could not memorise places.

4. Answering only when confident

The model outputs a probability, not just a class. Sorting roads from most to least confident and answering only for the top share trades coverage for accuracy. The remaining roads would be flagged for a human or left as unknown.

5. Does tag age matter?

Recently edited roads are scored more accurately. That alone does not show that old tags are worse labels. Recently edited roads might simply be easier.

Roads edited within a year before their photo score 96.9%; roads whose tag is 4+ years older than the photo score 95.1%. Roads edited only once score 94.4%.

6. Training on recent or cleaned labels

To test whether better labels help, we retrained four times with an identical setup: same starting model, 3,610 paved + 3,610 unpaved training roads, 5 passes, same held-out set. Only the choice of training roads differs.

  • Past year: roads last edited within a year of the OSM extract.
  • ±1 y of photo: roads last edited within a year of their aerial photo date.
  • Random: the baseline.
  • Cleaned: random roads minus the 1% whose tag an earlier model contradicted with over 90% confidence (359 of 34,483 checked).

Filtering training roads by edit date or by model disagreement made no measurable difference. The model is already robust to the few percent of wrong tags in training. More data per class helps far more: the full 44k training set gives 95.5% against about 94.6% here.

What the remaining errors look like

In a review of the top 24 confident errors, about 10 looked like wrong tags, about 4 were hidden roads, and the rest were unclear at this resolution. True accuracy is therefore somewhat higher than measured.

Recommendation

  • For labelling US roads, use the image model (3 versions × 4 flips) plus the OSM-tag model, and keep its probability.
  • Set a confidence cut-off to suit the use. Answering for 80% of roads gives about 99.6% agreement with OSM; flag the rest.
  • Skip label filtering by edit date. If more accuracy is needed, more training chips and a GPU are the next lever, not cleaner subsets.
  • Decide the final class grouping (where compacted and chipseal belong) before labelling the whole US. Changing it needs a half-day to one-day retrain.
